For your product, the “perfect university bio” should not be a Wikipedia-style description. It should be a **decision profile**: everything a student needs to decide whether this university is worth targeting, what the risks are, and what exactly they need to improve.

The structure should be like this:

# Perfect University Bio Structure

## 1. Hero Section

This is the first visual impression.

Should include:

```text
University name
Location
Campus photo / city photo
Logo
One-line positioning
Fit score for the student
Difficulty level
Application status
```

Example:

```text
Harvard University
Cambridge, Massachusetts, USA

Fit Score: 68%
Difficulty: Extreme
Best For: Economics, Government, CS, Liberal Arts
Main Risk: Your international achievement profile is not strong enough yet.
```

Photos matter, but don’t overdo it. Use 3–5 meaningful images:

* campus photo;
* city / environment photo;
* classroom / lab / student life photo;
* dorm / campus life photo;
* optional: map/location image.

Don’t use random aesthetic photos. Every image should help the student imagine life there.

---

# 2. Quick Decision Snapshot

This is the most important top card.

```text
Acceptance Rate: 3–5%
International Student Share: X%
Average SAT / ACT: X
Average IELTS / TOEFL: X
Estimated Annual Cost: $X
Scholarship Availability: High / Medium / Low
Application Deadline: X
Need-blind / Need-aware: X
Best-fit Majors: X, Y, Z
```

But here is the important thing: **acceptance rate alone is a weak metric**. It can be misleading because overall acceptance rate is not the same as international acceptance rate, program-level competitiveness, financial-aid competitiveness, or scholarship competitiveness. Your UI should show it, but not worship it.

Better:

```text
Overall Admit Rate: 3.4%
International Competitiveness: Extreme
Financial Aid Competitiveness: Very High
Program Difficulty: High
Your Current Readiness: Medium-Low
```

For US universities, the Common Data Set is one of the best sources because it standardizes information about admissions, enrollment, graduation, financial aid, and student demographics. The CDS initiative exists specifically to improve the quality and accuracy of college information. ([Common Data Set][1])

---

# 3. Fit For You

This is where your platform becomes better than normal catalogs.

Not:

```text
Harvard is a prestigious university.
```

That’s useless.

Better:

```text
Why this university fits you:
- Strong for your interest in economics + technology
- Good if you want interdisciplinary study
- Strong startup/research environment
- Strong global brand for future graduate school or entrepreneurship

Why it may not fit:
- Extremely competitive
- Weak applicants with no international-level proof are unlikely to stand out
- Essays and recommendation letters matter heavily
- Financial aid competition can be intense
```

This section must be personalized using memory.

For example:

```text
Because you said you dislike olympiads, your strongest path here would not be academic competitions. A better strategy would be startup competitions, research projects, regional-impact projects, and strong essays.
```

That is where your product feels intelligent.

---

# 4. Pros and Minuses

Most university websites only sell themselves. Your product should be honest.

## Pros

```text
- Strong global reputation
- Strong alumni network
- Excellent research environment
- Strong financial aid possibility
- Flexible academic structure
- High graduate outcomes
```

## Minuses

```text
- Extremely low acceptance rate
- High pressure environment
- Expensive without aid
- Very hard for average profiles
- Application requires strong narrative, not just certificates
- Some programs may be less practical than they look
- High competition from international applicants
```

This is important: **minuses make the product feel trustworthy**. If every university looks amazing, the platform becomes useless.

# 5. Admissions Data

This section should be very detailed, but evidence-based.

Include:

```text
Acceptance rate
Number of applicants
Number admitted
Number enrolled
Yield rate
International student percentage
Test score ranges
GPA / academic rigor indicators
English requirements
Required documents
Supplemental essays
Recommendation letters
Portfolio requirements
Interview requirements
Financial aid policy
Scholarship policy
Deadlines
Application platforms
```

For US universities, College Scorecard is useful because it provides data on costs, graduation rates, debt, and post-college earnings. ([collegescorecard.ed.gov][2]) College Navigator, from NCES, is also useful because it gives information about thousands of institutions and is designed for students, parents, and counselors. ([nces.ed.gov][3])

Your product should have an **Evidence Drawer** for every serious number:

```text
Acceptance Rate
Source: Common Data Set 2025–2026
Last checked: 16 June 2026
Confidence: High
```

If something is not found:

```text
International acceptance rate: Not publicly reported
Confidence: Unknown
```

Do not invent it.

---

# 6. Academic Profile

This is about whether the university is actually strong for the student’s major.

Include:

```text
Best programs
Departments
Research centers
Labs
Popular majors
Course flexibility
Double major / minor options
Undergraduate research availability
Internship connection
Study abroad options
Faculty/student ratio
Class size
```

For each student, show:

```text
Best-fit programs for you:
1. Business Analytics
2. Economics
3. Computer Science + Entrepreneurship
```

Then explain:

```text
Business Analytics fits your business + tech interest, but you need stronger math/data proof.
```

# 7. Student Life and Environment

This is underrated. Students don’t only choose stats.

Include:

```text
Campus vibe
City vibe
Weather
Safety
Transportation
Dorms
Food
Clubs
Startup ecosystem
International student community
Religious/cultural support
Sports
Social life
Workload intensity
```

But don’t write generic nonsense like:

> “The campus is vibrant and diverse.”

Instead:

```text
Good for students who want:
- intense academic environment;
- strong networking;
- research opportunities;
- ambitious peer group.

Bad for students who want:
- relaxed lifestyle;
- low-pressure environment;
- cheap city;
- simple admissions process.
```

# 8. Cost and Financial Aid

This needs to be brutally clear.

Include:

```text
Tuition
Housing
Meal plan
Books/materials
Insurance
Personal expenses
Travel estimate
Total annual cost
4-year estimated cost
Need-based aid
Merit scholarships
International eligibility
Average aid
Net price
Work-study / campus jobs
```

For US schools, College Scorecard and College Navigator are strong baseline sources for cost, graduation, debt, and outcomes data. ([collegescorecard.ed.gov][2])

UI should show:

```text
Sticker Price: $86,000/year
Possible Aid: Yes
Aid for internationals: Limited / Strong / Need-aware / Need-blind
Your financial risk: High
```

And explain what it means.

---

# 9. Outcomes

This is where many platforms are weak.

Include:

```text
Graduation rate
Retention rate
Median earnings
Employment outcomes
Graduate school placement
Top employers
Alumni network
Startup founders
Research output
Internship access
Career services quality
```

College Scorecard is especially useful here because it includes completion, debt, and earnings outcomes. ([collegescorecard.ed.gov][4])

But be careful: earnings data can be distorted by major, geography, financial-aid sample, and graduate school paths. So your product should say:

```text
Earnings data should be interpreted carefully. CS and engineering graduates can raise the average, while students going to graduate school may delay earnings.
```

This kind of honesty makes the platform feel serious.

# 10. Application Strategy

This is the section that will make your university bio unique.

For each university, show:

```text
What this university values
What kind of profile wins
What kind of profile is weak
How to position yourself
What essay angle may work
What achievements would improve your chance
What gaps should be fixed first
```

Example:

```text
Your best strategy:
Do not present yourself as “just a student interested in business.” That is too generic.

Better angle:
A student building technology projects for regional impact, with interest in business analytics and entrepreneurship.
```

This is much stronger than raw data.

# 11. Gap Radar For This University

This should be inside every university profile.

Example:

```text
Harvard Gap Radar

International achievement: Critical
Academic proof: High
Essay narrative: Critical
Leadership proof: Medium
Project impact: High
Recommendation strength: Unknown
Financial aid complexity: High
```

Then:

```text
Recommended fixes:
- Join 1 international startup competition
- Turn your current project into a measurable case study
- Build a strong essay theme around technology + regional impact
- Get one mentor/recommender who knows your work deeply
```

This connects university bio directly to roadmap.

# 12. Comparison Mode

Each university bio should be comparable.

Example:

| Metric             |             Harvard |           NUS |               HKU |
| ------------------ | ------------------: | ------------: | ----------------: |
| Fit Score          |                 68% |           82% |               78% |
| Difficulty         |             Extreme |     Very High |              High |
| Cost Risk          |                High |        Medium |            Medium |
| Scholarship Chance |          Low/Medium |        Medium |            Medium |
| Best For You       |               Reach | Strong Target |     Strong Target |
| Main Gap           | International proof |  Quant skills | Portfolio clarity |

This is much better than separate static pages.

# 13. Evidence Layer

Every bio should have a visible proof system.

For each fact:

```text
Data point:
Acceptance rate

Value:
3.4%

Source:
Common Data Set 2025–2026

Source type:
Official / institutional

Last checked:
16 June 2026

Confidence:
High
```

For weak data:

```text
Data point:
International acceptance rate

Value:
Not publicly reported

Confidence:
Unknown

Explanation:
The university reports overall acceptance rate but does not publish a separate international admit rate.
```

This is exactly how you reduce hallucination risk.

# 17. Most Important Principle

A perfect university bio should not answer:

> “What is this university?”

It should answer:

> **“Should I target this university, why, what are the risks, what proof supports this, and what should I do next?”**

That is the difference between a basic catalog and your product.
